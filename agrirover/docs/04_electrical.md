## 1. Power architecture

```
Bioenno 12.8 V 12 Ah (internal BMS)
  └─ Powerpole ─ in-line 10 A fuse holder (within 150 mm of the pack) ─ J1
       F1 10 A ─ Q1 P-FET (reverse polarity) ─ R2 5 mΩ ─┬─ VBAT
                              INA219 (V, I) ────────────┘
       VBAT ─ F2 5 A ─ J6 ─ Pololu D36V50F5 (5 V, 5.5 A) ─ USB-C ─ Raspberry Pi 5 (lidar, cameras from Pi USB)
       VBAT ─ F3 3 A ─ J7 ─ auxiliary (spare)
       VBAT ─ Q2 P-FET ─ VMOT ─ J5 ─ 2 × TB9051FTG carrier ─ motors      C5 470 µF
       VBAT ─ U3 TSR 1-2450 ─ +5 V (MCU module, encoders) ─ U4 AMS1117 ─ +3.3 V (INA219, I²C, pull-ups)
```

Typical battery current is 1.7 A (16 W mission average); both motors at the 3 A firmware limit plus all other loads
at their individual maxima sum to 11.3 A, which F1 is allowed to interrupt.

## 2. E-stop and motor-rail switching (the safety-relevant circuit)

* Q2 is a P-channel high-side switch. Its gate is pulled to the source by R3 (100 kΩ) — **off by default**.
* The gate is pulled down, turning Q2 on, only through the series chain R4 → **E-stop pole 1 (J14 pins 1–2, NC)** → Q3
  (2N7002) → GND, and Q3 conducts only while the MCU drives `MOTOR_EN` high (R6 holds it low during reset).
* Pressing the E-stop, a broken wire, an unplugged connector or an MCU reset all open the chain.
* C3 (2.2 µF) across gate–source holds Q2 on for about 0.4 s after the chain opens (τ = R3·C3 = 220 ms). In that
  window the firmware — informed immediately by **E-stop pole 2** on `ESTOP_SENSE` — ramps both wheels to zero at
  1.5 m/s² and brakes. Then the rail is gone whatever the software does. This is a stop-category-1 style behaviour
  (controlled stop, then power removal); it is **not** a certified safety function and has no performance level.
* The same RC gives a soft start (τ = R4·C3 = 22 ms) that limits the inrush into C5.
* `VMOT_SENSE` lets the firmware detect a missing rail (blown fuse, open E-stop chain, failed Q2) as fault `RAIL`.

Known weaknesses, accepted for revision A: single-channel architecture (one FET, one MCU input); a welded/shorted Q2
is detected only by `VMOT_SENSE` at the next disable; the motor drivers coast once the rail is gone, so the stopping
distance without the controlled-stop window depends on gearbox friction and must be measured (T-SAF-03).

## 4. The ARC-1 board

100 × 82 mm, 2 layers, 1 oz copper, 0.2 mm minimum clearance, 0.25 mm minimum track, 0.4 mm drills — within the
standard capability of low-cost fabs. 76 components (44 BOM lines), mostly through-hole connectors and 0805 passives;
smallest pitch is SOIC-8 (INA219) and the 1 mm Qwiic connectors. M3 mounting holes on a 92 × 74 mm pattern.

Track widths: 2.5 mm for the battery/motor path (IPC-2221: 6 A → ΔT ≈ 18 °C on 1 oz; 57 °C at the 10 A fuse rating —
a fault condition the fuse clears), 2.0/1.5 mm for the fused outputs, 0.6 mm logic power, 0.25 mm signals. Ground is
poured on both layers and stitched with 142 vias; ground pads connect solidly (no thermal reliefs) because battery
return current flows through them.

### How the board was produced — and what that means for trust

The schematic, placement and routing are **generated by scripts** (`electrical/pcb/build.sh`):

1. `gen_schematic.py` writes a KiCad 7 schematic using the stock KiCad symbol libraries; connectivity is expressed with
   net labels on short stubs at every pin.
2. KiCad itself (`kicad-cli`) extracts the netlist from that file; `check_netlist.py` compares it with the design
   intent and applies pin-type rules.
3. `board_place.py` loads stock KiCad footprints, assigns nets and checks for body overlaps.
4. `route.py` — a purpose-written two-layer A* maze router with exact clearance checking — routes every net.
5. `board_finish.py` adds tracks, vias and ground pours in KiCad and runs **KiCad's DRC**.

| Check | Tool | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Netlist extracted by KiCad equals design intent (51 nets, 10 no-connects) | kicad-cli + script | pass |
| Pin-type rules (drivers, undriven power inputs, unconnected pins), pull-up/pull-down presence, divider and gate-drive values | script | 0 errors, 0 warnings |
| Copper clearance, shorts, track width, hole and edge rules | KiCad DRC | 0 violations |
| Connectivity (unconnected pads) | KiCad DRC | 0 |
| Remaining DRC warnings | KiCad DRC | 76 × "footprint library not configured" (scripted environment), 6 × silkscreen overlap, 1 × 0.13 mm track stub |

**Not done, and required before ordering boards:** open the project in the KiCad GUI and run the interactive ERC
(KiCad 7's command line has none); have a second person review the schematic; verify every footprint against the
purchased part (especially the DPAK FETs, fuse holders and the Black Pill header order); review the autorouted layout
for current return paths. The board has not been fabricated, assembled or powered. Treat it as a reviewed-by-script
design, not a validated one.

| From | To | Cable |
|---|---|---|
| Battery Powerpole | in-line fuse 10 A → J1 | 16 AWG silicone, < 300 mm |
| J5 VMOT | both TB9051FTG `VIN`/`GND` | 18 AWG, star from J5 |
| TB9051FTG outputs | motor leads (red/black of the 6-wire cable) | 20 AWG, twisted |
| Motor encoder leads (green GND, blue Vcc, yellow A, white B) | J2 / J3 | JST-XH 4-pin |
| J4 | TB9051FTG carriers: PWM1, PWM2, EN, DIAG, OCM, VCC(3.3 V), GND | 12-way ribbon ≤ 150 mm |
| J6 | D36V50F5 VIN/GND; its 5 V output to a USB-C pigtail for the Pi | 18 AWG |
| J11 | Pi GPIO 14/15 (UART), GND | 3-wire, ≤ 200 mm |
| J14 | E-stop, 2 NC poles | 4-core, 22 AWG |
| J15 | bumper micro-switches in series | 2-core |
| Pi USB | lidar (USB adapter), 2 cameras | — |
| Pi I²C (Qwiic) | SCD41 → SHT45 → AS7341 in the mast shield | 500 mm + 2 × 100 mm; bus at 100 kHz |

Raspberry Pi 5 note: fed from a plain 5 V source the Pi limits USB peripheral current unless
`usb_max_current_enable=1` is set in `config.txt`; the D36V50F5's 5.5 A rating covers the Pi's 5 A budget.

TB9051FTG carrier note: the carrier's `OCC` pin is left at its default (over-current response = chopping). `EN` is
driven by `M_EN`; `ENB` is tied low on the carrier. Confirm against Pololu's pinout when wiring — the carrier pin
names are quoted from memory.

## 6. Firmware

`firmware/core` is hardware independent (protocol, PI + feed-forward speed control with slew limits, safety state
machine); `firmware/hal/stm32f411` is a bare-metal driver layer with a local register map. `make test` runs 31 checks
against a simulated motor plant under AddressSanitizer/UBSan; `make stm32` cross-compiles to a 6.6 kB image.
The STM32 layer **compiles and links but has never run on a microcontroller**; timer, ADC and I²C register usage needs
a bring-up session with a logic analyser (T-FW-02).
